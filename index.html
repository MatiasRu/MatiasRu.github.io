University profile

I am a Lecturer in Applied Mathematics at the University of Leicester. My research concerns the mathematics of wave propagation, with a particular interest in how geometry and material properties shape wave behaviour.

I combine rigorous analysis, asymptotic methods and numerical computation to study problems motivated by acoustics, electromagnetism and elasticity. My interests include partial differential equations, spectral theory, scattering and resonance, and nonlocal models.

Research

InterestsPublicationsTalks

My work connects the analysis of partial differential equations and integral operators with problems in wave physics, particularly plasmonics and resonant structures.

Nonlocal wave propagation

I investigate spatially dispersive media, where the response at a point depends on the field in a surrounding region. A central interest is how nonlocality changes plasmonic spectra, surface modes and the behaviour of fields near sharp geometries.

Spectral theory and integral operators

I study the spectra of boundary integral operators and their connection to resonances. Recent work uses block-Toeplitz operators and Wiener–Hopf methods to analyse self-similar chains and geometries with an accumulation point.

Asymptotic methods for resonant structures

I develop reduced models that retain the essential physics of wave problems. Examples include slender-body theories for metallic nanoparticles and nanometallic rings, linking geometry to resonant frequencies and electromagnetic response.

  1. A block-Toeplitz approach to the spectrum of the Neumann–Poincaré operator on self-similar chains

    Matias Ruiz

    arXiv:2608.30991 (2026)

    arXivPDF
    Research summary

    A block-Toeplitz representation connects the geometry of infinite self-similar chains with the essential spectrum of the Neumann–Poincaré operator. Wiener–Hopf factorisation and Szegő-type results describe isolated eigenvalues and large finite truncations.

  2. Spectral theory of plasmonic resonances in the nonlocal regime

    Hyundae Lee, Matias Ruiz and Sanghyeon Yu

    arXiv:2604.18461 (2026)

    arXivPDF
    Research summary

    A boundary integral formulation of the quasi-static hydrodynamic Drude model gives a rigorous account of nonlocal plasmonic resonances. Nonlocality changes the spectral structure, leaves only finitely many surface modes and regularises the singular behaviour associated with corners.

  3. Plasmonic resonances of slender nanometallic rings

    Matias Ruiz and Ory Schnitzer

    Physical Review B 105, 125412 (2022)

    JournalarXivPDF
    Research summary

    An asymptotic reduction turns the three-dimensional plasmonic eigenvalue problem for slender rings into a one-dimensional periodic problem. The model describes individual rings and coupled structures, including coaxial dimers and chains.

  4. Slender-body theory for plasmonic resonance

    Matias Ruiz and Ory Schnitzer

    Proceedings of the Royal Society A 475, 20190294 (2019)

    JournalOpen accessCode
    Research summary

    Matched asymptotic expansions produce an effective one-dimensional eigenvalue problem for slender metallic nanoparticles. The theory explains how particle shape controls surface-plasmon modes and near-infrared resonances.
